# Search direction: evidence and game default

Date: 2026-09-25. Direction is a code-level phenotype choice, not a biological law.

- In a field survey of **1,485 twining stems** across 17 sites, **92%** formed right-handed helices, called **anticlockwise** by the authors. This measures stems wrapping supports, not the free-search circumnutation of an ivy-like wall-root climber; viewing convention matters. [Edwards, Moles & Franks (2007), original study abstract hosted by James Cook University](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/2337/)
- In a controlled pea support-search study, among eight plants in the single-support group, two circumnutated clockwise, two counterclockwise and four in both directions; the double-support group also contained all three patterns. Pea *tendril* movements cannot prescribe one universal direction for an ivy-like stem. [Wang et al. (2023), §2.1](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10143786/fullTextXML)
- Thus **counterclockwise** is a defensible, stable **artistic default**, consistent with the broader twiner handedness tendency and the existing game default, but the evidence does not prove that English ivy searches counterclockwise. Keep both directions in a typed code API and its tests; remove the player-facing checkbox. Define clockwise as positive phase advance in the transported local search frame, rather than claiming an unambiguous world-camera or botanical viewpoint.

See also [the earlier support-search research](climbing_vine_support_search.md), §3 and sources S2/S7, which distinguishes searching from twining and warns against a universal circumnutation direction.
