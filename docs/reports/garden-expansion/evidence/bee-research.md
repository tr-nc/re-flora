# Garden bee houses: husbandry choice

Research date: 2026-10-26. This is a design note, not beekeeping advice or a claim of biological simulation.

## Retrieved primary source

Royal Horticultural Society, Olivia Drake, [5 easy ways to make a habitat for wild bees](https://www.rhs.org.uk/wildlife/how-to-make-habitats-for-wild-bees), retrieved in full as text. No photographs were imported.

- The introduction distinguishes managed honeybee colonies from solitary wild bees nesting in holes/cavities and recommends nearby flowering plants.
- “Easy bee hotel” describes untreated wood at least 10 cm deep, smooth holes 2–10 mm across, and a roof to protect the openings from rain. Its mounting instruction is: “Site your bee hotel in a sunny spot on a wall, fence or tree trunk, anywhere between 0.5m and 3m above ground.”
- “Bee post high-rise” describes drilled fence posts/dead trunks as another cavity-nesting habitat. The article also notes replacement/cleaning needs; a nesting block is not permanent maintenance-free conservation infrastructure.

## Decision and abstraction

Use a **roofed solitary-bee nesting house**, with nine recessed openings, mounted on a tree or a player-built pole. The toolbar says **Bee house**; internal `Hive` IDs preserve the requested feature name. This is **not a managed honeybee colony**, a honey factory, or a claim that all bee species use boxes. Roof/openings, stable attachment and nearby flowers are the source-informed elements. Sun direction and centimetre/metre dimensions are not imposed as game placement requirements.

Eight simulated residents per house and eight houses per garden are game budgets, not colony estimates. Generic yellow-striped bees, their relatively enlarged scale, second-scale trips, night return and fixed population are visual/gameplay choices. They do not model species identification, reproduction, nectar carrying capacity, parasites, nesting hygiene, overwintering, crop pollination rates or honey production. Missing hosts retire their cosmetic residents; houses cost no materials in this iteration. No player action is represented as real-world husbandry validation.
