## A/B control

Open `Debug Panel` -> `Character Walk` and toggle
`B: Smooth Microvoxel Walk (off = A: Original)`.

- Off is the default and preserves the original Rapier movement and 8-voxel camera-lag limit.
- On preserves intended XZ progress while Rapier follows climbable voxel edges, and lets the
  existing exponential camera smoother absorb a full permitted 16-voxel step.
- Both modes keep the current terrain collider and capsule position authoritative. B re-submits
  only unresolved horizontal motion through the same Rapier character controller, at most twice.
- Turning B off keeps the collision position unchanged and lets camera lag return inside the
  original 8-voxel envelope before resuming the exact A limit.

## Root-cause evidence

The report's camera hypothesis reproduced exactly: with a grounded 16-voxel rise at 60 Hz, the
original 8-voxel lag clamp produces an 8-voxel first-frame camera rise. B produces less than a
4-voxel first-frame rise with the same 14/s exponential response.

The real player-scale capsule also exposed an independent XZ issue. On a deterministic sequence of
1-voxel steps, a requested 1-voxel horizontal move could resolve to about 0.71 voxel. The capsule's
rounded contact normal made Rapier follow an upward tangent while preserving total translation
length, reducing horizontal progress. This is real collision movement loss, not camera Y motion.

## Acceptance boundary

The deterministic tests cover A/B semantics, XZ progress, the high-wall non-penetration guard, the
camera step response, and toggle continuity. The release smoke covers startup, the real Vulkan
surface/swapchain path, resource initialization, and shutdown. No visible game was launched, so
actual walking comfort, diagonal rough-terrain feel, cliff edges, low ceilings, jumping/landing,
and live terrain-edit feel still require user manual review. The extra collision-query cost on
frames that climb voxel edges has not received a release-mode performance benchmark.
